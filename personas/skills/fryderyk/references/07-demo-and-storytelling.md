# 07 — Demo and storytelling

**Load when:** the question is about running sales demos, structuring the pitch, what to say first, how to handle the call, the difference between a feature dump and a sale.

## The core idea: sales is storytelling

Real storytelling. Not bullshit. Real story — with a hero, a setup, a turn, a resolution. The kind that lets the listener join the dots themselves without effort. Their brain wants to do that. Yours has to lay the path.

> "Jeżeli miałbym dzisiaj wieczorem dać ci opcję: przeczytać książkę z historią, wstępem, bohaterami i wydarzeniami — albo przeczytać instrukcję obsługi pralki — gwarantuję, że wybierzesz książkę. W książce będziesz w stanie zrozumieć ciąg wydarzeń, utożsamisz się z bohaterami, automatycznie pomyślisz 'a co ja bym zrobił w tej sytuacji'. Czytając instrukcję pralki nie myślisz nic i co chwilę musisz się cofnąć, bo przestałeś kontaktować."

A lot of technical founders, when they pitch, recite the instruction manual. They list features. They explain how the system works. They give you a tour of the menu. *They are reading the instruction manual aloud*. The customer's brain checks out by minute three, and they say "let me think about it" — which is a polite no.

Tell a story instead.

## The shape of a demo (and outreach)

The same shape applies to both the first cold message and the demo itself, at different scales.

### 1. Anchor in their reality

Open by showing you understand what they already do. Reference what's on their Instagram, the offers they ran, the events they're throwing.

- "Słuchaj, widzę że robicie [X], wrzuciliście [Y], macie [Z]. Macie tu kupę aktywności, próbujecie różnych rzeczy."

Don't start with "we are a CRM platform". Start with their world.

### 2. Use *imagine if*

This is the move. Take what they're already doing, and reframe what your product enables as a natural extension.

- "Wyobraźcie sobie, że każdy klient, który kiedykolwiek odbił pieczątkę w karteczkę, byłby w waszej bazie danych. I że dzisiaj wieczorem mogliście wysłać wszystkim z nich maila i puscha z tą samą ofertą, którą wrzuciliście na Instagrama wczoraj — i która miała 10 lajków."
- "Wyobraź sobie, że wiesz dzisiaj, ilu stałych klientów nie wróciło do was od dwóch miesięcy. Z karteczką tego nie zrobisz. Mówię to dlatego, że właśnie to robimy."

This works because:

- It anchors in something they already understand (the analog version).
- It paints a vivid picture they can step into.
- It positions your product as a *natural upgrade*, not a foreign behaviour change.

### 3. Establish the "why" verbally — before the "what"

This is one of the most important moves in the whole demo. *Get the prospect to verbally agree with the fundamental why before you start showing them the product.*

For him: he wants the operator to say out loud, in their own words, that *the returning customer is the foundation of their business*. Almost every operator will agree — it's not controversial, the business literally lives or dies on retention.

But once they've said it out loud, you don't have to keep selling the why. When you show a feature, you don't have to keep stitching: "and this is why customer retention matters, and here's the reason…" — they already said it. You're now just answering the question they already endorsed.

This is enormous in pacing. You get to spend the rest of the demo on *how*, not on re-justifying *why*.

### 4. Show features as natural answers, not as a menu

Every feature you demo is now answering a question they themselves planted. You don't have to keep selling — they're already inside the story.

- "OK to dlatego pokażę ci, jak możesz dziś rano w 30 sekund napisać do wszystkich klientów, którzy nie byli u was od miesiąca."
- "To jest dlatego masz tu widok — ilu klientów wróciło w tym tygodniu, ile nowych, ile straciliście."

Each feature lands as a *that's exactly what I'd want*, not as a *huh, interesting, what else?*.

### 5. Close the loop — back to the start

Like a film that starts with a scene that doesn't make sense, and you spend the movie figuring out why it mattered — your demo opens with the analog reality (stamp card, "ask in store" sign) and closes by *replacing* it.

- "Słuchaj, dzisiaj masz tę karteczkę na pieczątki, która nie daje ci ani danych, ani komunikacji, ani widoku na powracalność. Pokazałem ci, że tu możesz robić to, to i to. Czyli tę karteczkę zamieniasz na to — i to się wdraża w 3 dni robocze."

That return-to-the-opening creates the *look how far we've come* feeling. It's deeply satisfying for the listener. The story closes. They're sold.

## Speak the language

Don't drop SaaS jargon. Don't talk about CRMs, engagement, retention metrics, churn — those are *your* words. Their words are: "stali klienci", "klienci co wracają", "promocja na kanapkę", "akcja na nowe menu", "rozdaj voucher". Use theirs. Translate yours into theirs every time.

> "Już to robicie. Już to próbujecie. Po prostu zróbmy to lepiej."

That phrase carries enormous weight: it respects them, it frames you as an enabler rather than a saviour, and it lowers the felt cost of change.

## What the customer is NOT

The buyer is not someone sitting in an office about to spend an hour comparing your product to two competitors. They are someone running fires in the morning, jumping on your demo, then immediately going back to running fires.

So:

- The demo cannot rely on them re-watching a 45-minute recording later.
- The demo cannot rely on them remembering every feature.
- The demo has to deliver a clear *story* that they could re-tell to their team in two sentences after the call ends.

That story is what gets sold internally. Make sure they leave with it.

## Pre-build the responses to pushback

Before the demo, you should have already mapped the standard objections (see `08-pushback-handling.md`). They will come. Don't be surprised by them. Have the *re-framed response* ready as part of the story flow, not as a defensive afterthought.

## What the demo is for, really

The demo is not for selling. The demo is for getting them to say "tell me more" again — at the next level. "OK what would it look like for *us*?" — that's the question you want. From there, follow-ups and packaging do the rest. (See `09-followups-and-closing.md`.)

## Concrete metrics he tracks

Their current demo-to-close conversion is roughly 60–70%. Note he obsessed over this. Once the demo flow was nailed, he found the leakage *after* the demo, not in it — which sent him into followup discipline (see `09-followups-and-closing.md`).

If your demo conversion is under 30%, the demo itself is broken — you're probably reading the instruction manual.
