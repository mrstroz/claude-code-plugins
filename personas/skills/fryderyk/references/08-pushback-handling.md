# 08 — Pushback handling

**Load when:** the question is about handling objections, what to do when a prospect says "we tried that, didn't work", root-cause analysis on customer complaints, pre-building responses.

## Don't take pushback at face value

This is the single most important move. When a prospect tells you why something won't work, *they are giving you a problem*. Your job is not to accept the surface-level objection and let the deal die — your job is to drill into *why* that problem exists, and then *solve* it.

The default founder reaction is wrong on both sides:

- **Bad reaction 1**: "OK they said no, time to move on." You're losing deals that were solvable.
- **Bad reaction 2**: "They said apps don't work, so I'll stop pitching apps." You're letting a single bad data point hijack your product strategy.

The right reaction: take the objection apart.

## Worked example: "we tried apps, they didn't work"

Common objection in hospitality: "We tried some technology platform / app, it didn't catch on. The staff hated it. Customers ignored it. We're skeptical."

Don't accept "apps don't work" as the conclusion. Drill:

- *Dlaczego nie działało?* → because customers had a hard time signing up.
- *Dlaczego mieli problem z rejestracją?* → because the onboarding flow required email + password + verification at the counter, with a queue behind them.
- *Kiedy to się działo?* → at the moment of purchase.
- *Co z tym zrobić?* → maybe the customer should be able to participate as a *guest user* in 5 seconds at the counter, then convert to a registered account *while walking back to work or waiting for the coffee*.

That's the answer. The objection isn't "apps don't work" — the objection, decomposed, is "the onboarding pattern we tried added friction at the worst possible moment". Your product, designed correctly, can absolutely solve that.

So the move is:

1. **Don't argue with the objection.** "OK, rozumiem. Możesz mi opowiedzieć, co konkretnie nie zadziało?"
2. **Drill on cause.** Why? Why specifically? What was the actual breaking point?
3. **Match your product (or build the product) to solve the *underlying* problem.**
4. **Come back with the answer.** Not in this call necessarily — but in a follow-up. "Słuchaj, długo myślałem nad tym, co powiedziałeś. Wydaje mi się, że właściwy problem to było X. U nas to rozwiązaliśmy tak…"

## The founder's actual job here

> "Twoja robota jest zrozumieć problem i znaleźć rozwiązanie. Ty biorąc ten feedback — nie możesz go wziąć tak face value. 'U nas problem jest taki, że nie działają aplikacje' — *dlaczego*? Czemu? I wchodzisz głębiej i rozwiązujesz problem."

This applies in sales conversations, in customer interviews, and in product strategy. The discipline is the same: do not accept the surface complaint as the root.

## Pre-build the pushback catalogue

Before you have any volume of sales conversations, you can predict 80% of the objections you'll hear. Sit down and list them:

- "We tried apps, didn't work."
- "Our customers aren't tech-savvy."
- "It's too expensive."
- "We don't have time to implement it."
- "Our staff won't use it."
- "We're locked in with [competitor / POS provider]."
- "We're too small for something like this."
- "Loyalty doesn't matter for our type of business."
- "We don't have email addresses of customers."
- "It looks complicated."

For each, write the *root cause* version of the objection, and the response that addresses it. Now you walk into every demo armed.

This is part of the prep, not improvisation. You will use the same 8–10 responses constantly.

## Asking for pushback proactively

A useful move: invite pushback into the conversation. Don't wait for it to land as a polite "let me think about it".

- "Co was niepokoi najbardziej, jeżeli mielibyśmy iść z tym dalej?"
- "Kiedy pomyślicie 'to się u nas nie sprawdzi' — co przychodzi wam do głowy?"
- "Co was kiedyś sparzyło, jeżeli chodzi o rozwiązania technologiczne?"

This pulls the objection out into the open, where you can address it, instead of letting it kill the deal silently after the call.

## Pushback as product input

Every objection is also a piece of product feedback. Track them. Patterns matter.

If 8 out of 10 prospects raise the same concern, it's not a sales-pitch problem — it's a product problem. Fix it. The next 10 conversations get easier.

The example he gave: the early observation that customers struggled with sign-up flows at the counter led to building guest-user onboarding, where the customer can scan and earn a stamp in ~5–10 seconds without registering, and convert to a full account later. That came directly from the pushback "people couldn't sign up". The pushback became a feature.

## The relationship between pushback and trust

When you take pushback seriously — drill on it, come back with a real answer, show that you've thought about *their* specific problem and not generic objections — you build trust faster than a slick demo ever does.

It signals: *I'm not here to push a product on you. I'm here because I want to actually solve your problem.* That's how SMB owners decide who to work with. Not by feature lists.

## What he says in the room

- "Aha, OK. To powiedz mi więcej. *Czemu* to się nie sprawdziło?"
- "Dobra, biorę to. Daj mi 2 dni — wrócę z tym, jak my to widzimy."
- "Słuchaj, większość biznesów co miały ten sam problem, miały go z tego powodu. U was też?"
- "Nie chcę cię na siłę przekonywać. Powiedz mi co bardziej cię niepokoi, to się temu przyjrzymy."

Calm. Curious. Not defensive. Not pushy. Empathic to their actual experience.
