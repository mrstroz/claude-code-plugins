# 06 — Outreach

**Load when:** the question is about cold outreach, finding prospects, channels, what to say in the first message, prospecting for businesses that don't live on LinkedIn.

## The hard truth about SMB hospitality outreach

The standard B2B outreach playbook assumes your prospects are office workers who:

- Live on LinkedIn.
- Check email every 5 minutes.
- Are listed in scrapable databases.
- Have personal emails on Apollo / ZoomInfo / similar.

None of that is true for SMB hospitality.

- Owners of local cafés and bakeries don't sit on LinkedIn.
- There's no clean scrapable database of personal emails of small operators.
- Even getting `info@` / `hello@` addresses is hard — and `info@` outreach goes nowhere anyway.
- Most aren't even on LinkedIn with their full name.

So the standard tools (mass mailing, LinkedIn automation, scraped databases) — *no way*. They simply don't apply.

Bad news: you have to build your own outreach channels. Good news: because it's hard, once it works, *you have defensibility no one can copy quickly*.

## Where do they actually live?

The discipline is: find the platforms where your prospects spend time, then build a way to reach them there.

For hospitality SMB: **Instagram** is the strongest one. Why:

- The people running these businesses (or someone in their team) manages the Instagram. So the social handle is essentially a contact channel to a decision-maker or a near-decision-maker.
- Having an Instagram at all is a filter: it tells you they care minimally about being seen, and have at least dipped a toe into the digital side of running a business. A bakery without any Instagram in 2025 is probably not your customer yet.
- Their content tells you what they're already trying — offers, new menu items, events, collabs. You can speak directly to *what they're already doing*.

(Other channels exist too — local industry forums, sector-specific Facebook groups, walk-ins for high-conviction targets, supplier referrals. He won't go into detail on his specific channels — those are part of the *play your own game* moat. But the principle: go where they live, not where the SaaS playbook says to go.)

## Walk-ins as a tactic

Walk-ins (door-to-door) feel right intuitively, but they're expensive and slow. He's clear:

> Walking in is fine, it's a fun signal of intent. But it's *cholernie* time-consuming. It can be an element of the mix, but it cannot be the engine. With tens of thousands of locations to address, you will never scale on shoe leather.

Walk-in is a tactic for *closing*, not for *opening*. Use it to push something across the line, not to start the conversation.

## The target person inside the business

You're not blasting `info@`. You're identifying who in the business is most likely to even read what you send, and going to them.

Best guess for the "digitally savvy" person in a typical small-bakery setup: the person who manages their Instagram. They:

- Already think in digital terms (they think about audience, engagement, content).
- Are often younger / next-generation in the family.
- May not be the final decision-maker, but they're a route in — and they care about the digital story.

If you can talk to them, you've shortcut the conversation. They'll do internal selling for you.

## What goes in the first message

Don't write the standard sales speech. Write a message that gets to "tell me more" — nothing more, nothing less. The first message has one job: provoke curiosity. Sell later.

A good opening — Fryderyk-style:

1. **Anchor on something they just did.** "Wrzuciliście tę ofertę z nowym menu 10% na Instagrama. Widziałem 10 lajków."
2. **Imagine if.** "Wyobraźcie sobie, że moglibyście wysłać tę samą ofertę mailem i pushem do wszystkich klientów, którzy kiedykolwiek odbili u was pieczątkę. Tak to robi McDonald's, Żabka. Robimy to dla [X] biznesów."
3. **Visual proof.** Drop a screenshot of how it could look on their phone. A case study. A 30-second video.
4. **Stop.** Don't try to close. Don't pitch the product. End with "Daj znać, jak chcesz pogadać 15 minut".

Things to avoid in the first message:

- Long paragraphs of feature descriptions.
- Generic "we help businesses grow" framing.
- A calendar link before they've said yes to talking.
- Asking the prospect to read an instruction manual.

## Why visual content matters so much

Gastronomy is a visual industry. The people in it eat with their eyes and sell with the eye too. They post on Instagram because their world is visual. So your outreach has to match.

Blocks of text — *zero impact*. Screenshots, short videos, case studies presented visually — these are the *language* of the industry. Match it.

A 30-second video showing what their loyalty program could look like on a customer's phone beats two paragraphs about "real-time data and engagement metrics" every single time.

## What he's NOT going to tell you

He's deliberately *not* sharing the specific outreach channels they discovered that work for them. Those are part of why they have defensibility while competitors are stuck running SaaS playbooks. When asked, he says: "tutaj nie wchodzę bardzo szczegółowo, bo to są takie rzeczy, które myślę, że nas wyróżniają". You can respect that and still extract the principles:

- Find where they live.
- Match their visual language.
- Anchor on what they're already doing.
- Filter for minimum digital savviness.
- Target the digital-savvy person inside the business.
- First message just earns "tell me more".

## Sales = storytelling, from message one

The outreach message is already storytelling. Don't waste it on a feature dump. Set the scene, hook the curiosity, leave them wanting the next chapter. (See `07-demo-and-storytelling.md` — this is the same principle applied to the first message.)

> "Jak zacznę książkę i ten wstęp jest do dupy, no to często ją porzucasz. Twój pierwszy pitch musi być właśnie taki — dobrze postawiony wstęp, że ktoś chce dalej czytać."
